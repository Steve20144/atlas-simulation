# SITL tuning session, 2026-09-14

Scenario `atlas_weighed_p26_side30`: 11.74 kg from the dashboard weights, inertia 0.58 / 0.67 /
1.06 kg m² (FRD), foils 50/55/60/65 inner to outer, nose fans leaned 30 deg sideways (fan 8
right, fan 9 left), hover pitch 26. gz sim Harmonic, PX4 v1.17.0, hover_run.sh with 55 s hover
at 2.5 m, one parameter group per flight. Numbers from `scripts/tune_table.py` over the hover
window (3 s after above 1 m to 3 s before). Logs `exports/logs/runN_*.ulg`.

**Run 11, the export did not reproduce the live result.** Same gains in the log, yet the pitch
integrator settled at 0.053 again and the liftoff dip was back. The Gazebo exporter took the
fan's motorConstant from the CA_ROTORn_CT parameter, override included, so the baked 28.84 N
also weakened the two simulated nose fans and the 13 percent lift mismatch returned. Live in
run 10 only the allocator had changed. Fixed in `vectra/export/gazebo.py` and
`gazebo_classic_hitl.py`: physics now uses `Scenario.fan_ct_physical` (fan curve times foil
efficiency); the override reaches the airframe and the board only. Lesson for the loop: a live
parameter changes one thing, a baked one may change two; confirm from a relaunch every time.

Units: attitude peak to peak in degrees, rate error RMS in deg/s, torque normalised (1 = full
authority), drift is the largest distance from the window mean.

## What each run taught

**Run 1, the integrator lock.** Attitude within a degree, torque demand near zero, yet the
vehicle accelerated backwards to 17 m/s in the body frame. The attitude setpoint sat at the
tilt limit (pitch -7.8 deg) while the aircraft held +2.6 deg. `control_allocator_status` live:
`torque_setpoint_achieved False`, unallocated torque [-0.0016, -0.0026, -0.0143]. PX4 freezes
each rate integrator in the direction of any unallocated torque above FLT_EPSILON
(`MulticopterRateControl.cpp:200-215`), so all three integrators were locked and the loops ran
on P alone against a steady trim moment. The metrics had predicted it: `exact False` for the
sideways nose lean at every hover pitch, residual of order 1e-2 Nm with no motor at a limit,
identical for CA_METHOD 0, 1 and 2, so it is the least-squares solution itself, not clipping.

**Run 2, the fix.** Declaring the nose fans without side lean to the allocator (`CA_ROTOR8_AY 0
CA_ROTOR9_AY 0`) gave an exact trim, `torque_setpoint_achieved True`, unallocated torque zero,
pitch rate error from 7.6 to 1.0 deg/s and drift from 270 m to 3.5 m with the same gains. The
physical lean stays in the sim and on the aircraft; the pitch integrator settles at about 0.05
and the yaw integrator at 0.08 to 0.15 to carry the unmodelled moments.

**Runs 3 to 7, the yaw that was not a gain problem.** Every yaw gain combination between one
seventh of the rule and the rule itself left the heading swinging hundreds of degrees. The run 7
log settled it: the flight task's own yaw setpoint (`trajectory_setpoint.yaw`) was rotating,
following the bearing from the vehicle to its position setpoint whenever the aircraft was a few
metres off it, and the aircraft was tracking that setpoint faithfully (yaw within 15 deg of the
setpoint, rate within 10 deg/s). `MPC_YAW_MODE 0` (towards waypoint) does that in loiter; the
drift moves the bearing, the heading change upsets the position loop, and the two chase each
other at 0.12 Hz, which is also what the roll and pitch swings were (setpoint peak to peak 13 deg
against attitude 20 deg). Yaw plant fit from the log: 2.97 rad/s² per unit torque with the
allocator believing about 1.8, sign correct, no allocator saturation. Fix: `MPC_YAW_MODE 5`
(yaw fixed), run 8.

**Yaw integrator limit.** With the weak yaw gains of run 2 the yaw integrator sat at its
0.15 limit. The unmodelled yaw moment of the leaned nose fans needs about 0.08 to 0.15 of the
yaw authority in hover, so keep `MC_YR_INT_LIM` at 0.3.

**Runs 8 to 10, heading fixed, then the liftoff dip.** With `MPC_YAW_MODE 5` the heading held
within 6 deg and the position loop settled to ±0.2 m after about 10 s at altitude. What was left
was the first five seconds: the aircraft pitched 5 deg nose down at liftoff, slid 2.4 m sideways
and the pitch integrator climbed to 0.05 and stayed there (run 9). The allocator, told the nose
fans are vertical, expected 33.3 N of lift from each while the leaned fans deliver
33.3 x cos 30 = 28.8 N vertically; the nose sagged until the integrator found the missing moment.
Scaling `CA_ROTOR8_CT` and `CA_ROTOR9_CT` to 28.84 (run 10) halved the dip, cut the slide to under
1 m, took the pitch integrator to about -0.01 and the hover drift from 2.7 to 1.3 m. The sideways
slide itself is physical: pitch torque from the nose fans is differential thrust on two fans
leaning opposite ways, so it always comes with a net side force. That is a property of this
geometry, not of the gains, and it will be there on the aircraft.

**Baked set** (scenario `atlas_weighed_p26_side30`, `control.px4_params_override`):
CA_ROTOR8_AY 0, CA_ROTOR9_AY 0, CA_ROTOR8_CT 28.84, CA_ROTOR9_CT 28.84, MPC_YAW_MODE 5,
MC_YAWRATE_P 0.6, MC_YAWRATE_I 0.06, MC_YR_INT_LIM 0.3, MC_YAW_P 0.6, on top of the earlier
overrides (MPC_THR_HOVER 0.384, MC_PITCHRATE_I 0.5, MC_PR_INT_LIM 0.6, MC_RR_INT_LIM 0.3,
MPC_TILTMAX_AIR 8 and the MPC_* set). Roll and pitch rate gains stayed at the rule values
(0.0705 and 0.1449): their rate errors were 0.2 to 0.3 deg/s RMS and torque peaks under 0.02,
nothing to tune there in hover.

## Rules confirmed on this aircraft

- Before touching gains, probe `torque_setpoint_achieved` mid hover. False means fix the
  allocator geometry first.
- Attitude and position swings at 0.1 to 0.2 Hz with the setpoints swinging too are not rate
  loop problems. Look at the setpoint source (flight task yaw mode, position loop gains).
- A ten-fan geometry with tilted fans has small, asymmetric yaw authority; the yaw loop can hold
  a fixed heading but not chase a rotating one while the position loop is still settling.
